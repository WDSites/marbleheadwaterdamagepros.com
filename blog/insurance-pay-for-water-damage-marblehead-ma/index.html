How to Get Insurance to Pay for Water Damage in Marblehead, MA

No one can promise a claim will be paid, but a well documented claim gives your insurer what it needs to decide. This is the order of work that tends to matter most.

By the Marblehead Water Damage Pros team · Updated 2026-10-29

Insurance companies do not pay for water damage because a homeowner asked loudly. They pay, or decline, based on the cause of the loss, the policy language and the evidence in the claim file. You control the evidence, and that is where you have the most influence.

This walkthrough covers what to do in the first hours, what to gather over the next few days and what to ask your insurer. It does not predict outcomes, because every policy and every loss is different.

Hour one: stop the loss before you start the paperwork

Most policies expect you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage after a loss. Shutting off the main water valve, switching off a failed appliance or covering a roof opening with a tarp counts. So does calling a restoration company to remove water and start drying, and that is typically a cost your insurer will want to see receipts for.

If electricity or gas is involved, stop there. Call 911 or the utility first, then deal with the water.

Photograph and video everything before you move it

The Massachusetts flood assistance page advises photographing the outside of the property to show damage or flooding, and photographing the inside, including damaged items and the height of floodwater if the property flooded. Follow that for any water loss, not only floods. Take wide shots of each room, then close-ups of wet drywall, stained ceilings, ruined flooring and the failed fixture or pipe.

A slow walk-through video narrated out loud (room, what happened, what is damaged) is useful backup. Keep the original files with their date stamps intact.

Build an inventory while the memory is fresh

List damaged belongings room by room: what it was, roughly when and where you bought it, and what you think it cost. Photos of model and serial plates help for appliances and electronics. If you already have a home inventory, the state flood page suggests using it to present your claim to the adjuster.

Keep damaged items until the adjuster has seen them

It is tempting to haul a soaked sofa to the curb to clear space. Resist that unless sanitary or safety reasons force you to, and photograph it first. The state guidance tells homeowners to separate damaged items from undamaged ones and arrange them for the adjuster's inspection.

If something has to go, such as sewage-affected carpet or saturated drywall that is sagging, document it with photos and tell your insurer before disposal. Ask whether a sample, a cut square of carpet or a photo of the label is enough.

Mitigation receipts and why they matter

Mitigation means the work that stops a loss from growing: water extraction, drying, removal of unsalvageable materials and protection of what remains. Your claim file benefits from a written scope, dated invoices, a list of equipment placed and the daily moisture readings. Our page on water mitigation describes what that record usually includes.

Scope and price should be explained before work starts. If a company will not put its scope in writing, that is worth weighing before you sign.

What to ask when you call your insurer

  • Is this type of loss covered under my policy, and what is my deductible for it?
  • Is there a limit or sublimit for water damage, water backup or mold?
  • What documents and photos do you need from me, and by what date?
  • Will an adjuster visit, and should I keep the damaged materials until then?
  • Can I start emergency drying now, and what records do you need to see?
  • How will I receive updates, and who is my single point of contact?

Write the answers down as they are given. If a representative says something that conflicts with your policy, ask for it in writing. Your policy declarations and endorsements are the controlling documents, so read those too.

Safety note

Do not enter a flooded basement with live outlets or an active furnace below the waterline. A ceiling holding water can collapse, so keep clear of bulging drywall and do not poke it from underneath. Assume sewage or flood water contains contaminants and wear gloves and boots if you must handle anything.

Marblehead note: what the state says about covered water damage

Mass.gov states that a standard policy usually covers water damage that is "sudden and accidental," such as a burst pipe, and that your insurer will investigate the cause to confirm coverage. The same page says flood damage is not covered by a standard policy.

For a Marblehead homeowner, the first practical move is to be able to name the cause precisely, because coverage often hinges on it. A pipe that failed overnight and storm water that rose from outside are treated very differently, so write down what you saw and when.

What affects the size of a water damage claim

Cause of loss

Sudden failures and gradual leaks are treated differently by most policies.

Extent of wet materials

The more drywall, flooring and contents affected, the larger the scope.

Water category

Contaminated water can require removing materials that clean water would not.

Your deductible and limits

These are set in your policy and can change what you actually receive.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I get my insurance company to pay for water damage?

No one can guarantee payment, but you improve your position by stopping the loss, photographing everything, keeping damaged items for the adjuster and saving every mitigation receipt. Report the loss promptly and ask your insurer what it needs.

Should I call my insurer before or after cleanup starts?

Call as soon as it is safe to do so, and ask whether you can begin emergency water removal and drying right away. Most policies expect you to protect the property from further damage, so waiting for an adjuster before stopping the water is usually a mistake.

What photos should I take for a water damage claim?

Take wide shots of every affected room, close-ups of damaged materials and contents, the source of the leak and the exterior of the home. Mass.gov specifically advises photographing the outside and inside, including damaged items and floodwater height.

Do I need to keep the damaged items?

Keep them until your insurer's adjuster has seen them or told you otherwise, unless they are unsafe or sanitary reasons require disposal. Photograph anything you discard and tell your insurer.

Will my insurer pay for emergency mitigation?

Many policies cover reasonable emergency mitigation when the underlying loss is covered, but that depends on your policy and the cause. Ask directly, and keep invoices and drying logs either way.

Can a restoration company deal with my insurer for me?

The claim is between you and your insurer. A restoration company can provide scope, photos, invoices and moisture logs for you to submit, but you should read your policy and speak to your insurer yourself.

Does the state of Massachusetts say anything about which water damage is covered?

Mass.gov says a standard policy usually covers water damage that is sudden and accidental and that flood damage is not covered by a standard policy. Your own policy language is what decides your claim.
