Why one house gets two very different mold quotes

A price for mold remediation is the sum of a scope, and scopes vary more than people expect. One company may quote to clean a visible patch. Another may quote to open the wall, contain the room, dry the structure and rebuild. Both can be honest, and the totals will not compare.

This page gives no dollar figures on purpose. Online averages hide the variables that matter in an older Massachusetts house, and a number that does not match your scope is worse than no number. The useful skill is reading what each quote includes.

Cost factors

Size of the affected area

More square footage means more labor, more disposal and more time under containment. The DPH guide points to about 10 square feet as the line where professionals take over from homeowners.

Material type

Porous materials such as drywall, insulation and carpet are discarded when moldy, while framing and concrete are cleaned in place. A finished basement has more to remove than a bare one.

Access and layout

Crawl spaces, tight attics and rooms with stairs to carry debris down take longer. Tall ceilings may need scaffolding.

Containment and air control

Plastic barriers, negative air and HEPA filtration add equipment and setup time. The scope should state what is used and why.

Testing and verification

Post-work checks cost extra if they are in the plan. The state guide steers homeowners away from lab sampling, so ask what each test would actually prove.

The rebuild is the item people forget. Removing wet drywall is one line, while closing the wall, insulating, taping, priming and painting can be another contractor's job. Insurance is a general question: sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, gradual leaks and neglect often are not, and flood damage is usually excluded without separate flood coverage, so check your policy.

Costs here follow the room, the materials and the moisture, so call the dispatch line and describe what you have.

Reading a written scope, item by item

A good scope reads like a list of tasks with boundaries. Each line should say where, how much and with what method. Vague words such as 'treat the area' or 'clean affected materials' leave room for the price to change after work starts.

Why quotes differ even when the house is the same

Three differences cause most of the gap. Companies scope different amounts of demolition, they plan different levels of containment, and some include drying and rebuild while others stop at cleaning. A low bid that omits the moisture fix may be the most expensive choice if the mold returns.

Ask each company to bid the same defined scope, then ask separately what extras they would recommend and why. That puts the differences on paper where you can judge them.

How to compare scopes side by side

1

Get the scope in writing

Ask for itemized work, materials, equipment, duration and exclusions. Verbal assurances do not help when a dispute starts.

2

Match the units

Check that each bid covers the same rooms, the same demolition boundaries and the same level of containment.

3

Check the standard

Ask whether the crew follows ANSI/IICRC S520, the published standard for mold remediation, and how it affects the plan.

4

Look for the water fix

Confirm who corrects the moisture source, and whether drying with water mitigation equipment is included.

5

Confirm payment terms

The DPH guide suggests paying in installments and keeping the last payment back until the work has been inspected.

  • What happens if the crew opens a wall and finds more growth than expected, and who approves the added work in writing?
  • Is the price for the described scope fixed, or does it change with hours and materials?
  • Which line items are optional, and what does skipping each one risk?
  • What does the crew do with the drywall and insulation it removes, and who carries it out of the house?
  • How long will the containment and equipment stay, and when are moisture readings taken?

A company that cannot explain a line item in plain words is telling you something. The best bid is the one whose scope you understand, not simply the lowest total.

Marblehead note: no state license means the scope is your safeguard

The Massachusetts DPH homeowner mold guide states that no licenses are required to conduct mold remediation in Massachusetts. That means a business name or van lettering tells you little about what a company will actually do on your Marblehead job.

Compare written scopes, ask for the standard being followed (ANSI/IICRC S520 is published for mold remediation), and ask for the company's references. If the property is in Swampscott or Lynn, the same questions apply there.

Source: Massachusetts Department of Public Health homeowner mold guide, mass.gov.

What a fair estimate leaves out on purpose

An honest early estimate often separates what is known from what is not. The extent of hidden growth cannot be seen until a wall is opened, so the scope may include a change process, such as written approval before extra demolition. That is better than a flat price that quietly assumes the best case.

If you have questions about health, take them to a physician. A remediation estimate is about materials, air control and drying, and it should not make medical promises. Read any such language in a bid as a warning sign.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does mold remediation cost in Marblehead?

It depends on area, materials, access, containment, testing and rebuild, so no responsible number exists without a scope. The fastest way to get one for your property is to call the dispatch line, describe the rooms and materials involved, and ask for a written scope.

Why is my second mold quote so much higher than my first?

Look at the scope. The higher bid may include containment, demolition, drying or rebuild that the lower bid leaves out. Ask each company to price the same defined work, then compare the differences.

Does homeowners insurance cover mold remediation?

It depends on the policy and the cause. Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, gradual leaks and neglect often are not, and flood damage is usually excluded without separate flood insurance. Ask your insurer before work starts.

Should testing be part of the price?

Only if you know what it proves. The state guide does not endorse lab sampling. If a scope includes verification, ask who performs it and what counts as a pass.

Do mold remediation companies need a license in Massachusetts?

Per the MA DPH guide, Massachusetts does not require a license to do mold remediation. That is why you should compare written scopes and ask about the S520 standard, references and insurance.

Is the rebuild included in a Marblehead mold quote?

Not always. Many quotes cover removal and cleaning only, with drywall, paint and flooring as a separate rebuild. Ask the company to say in writing what is included and who handles each trade.
