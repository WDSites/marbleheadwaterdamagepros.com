Water damage restoration is the work of stopping a water problem, removing standing water, drying the structure and repairing what could not be saved. In Marblehead it covers burst pipes, failed water heaters, roof and window leaks, sump pump failures, and storm water that reaches a basement. The goal is simple: get the building dry before mold and rot start.

Marblehead Water Damage Pros is a dispatch network. When you call, we connect you with licensed and insured restoration professionals who serve Marblehead and the nearby towns of Swampscott, Salem, Lynn, Peabody and Beverly.

The first hour: what to do while you wait

Whatever is wet, the next sixty minutes matter more for what you protect than for what you fix. If water is still coming in, find the source and stop it. For a plumbing leak that means the main shutoff valve, usually near where the water line enters the basement or utility room.

  • Shut off the water at the main valve, or at the fixture valve if the leak is a single toilet, sink or appliance.
  • Cut power to the wet area only if the panel is dry and safe to reach. If water is near outlets, the panel or anything sparking, stay out and call 911 or the utility first.
  • Lift valuables, rugs, electronics and paper off wet floors, and move furniture legs onto foil or plastic.
  • Keep people and pets out of any water that may have come from a drain, toilet or outside flooding.
  • Take photos and short video of the damage before anything is moved. Your insurer will ask for them.

Then call the dispatch line. A dispatcher will ask where the water came from, how far it has spread and what materials are wet, so the right crew and equipment are matched to the job.

Water categories and what each one means

Restoration crews classify water by how contaminated it is. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the published industry standard for professional water damage restoration, and it uses three categories. The category sets what can be dried and kept and what has to be removed.

Water categories under the S500 standard
CategoryWhat it meansWhat is done
Category 1Clean water from a broken supply line, a failed fixture or rain through a clean sourceExtract the water, set air movers and dehumidifiers, and monitor drying until the materials read dry
Category 2Gray water from a dishwasher or washing machine, or clean water that has sat long enough to pick up contaminationExtract, clean and treat affected surfaces, and remove porous materials such as carpet padding that cannot be cleaned
Category 3Grossly contaminated water such as sewage backup or flood water from outsideRemove affected porous materials, clean and disinfect what stays, and dry the structure with protective gear on the crew

Clean water does not stay Category 1 forever. Left for a day or two, it can degrade to Category 2 and need more work to fix, which is why calling early helps.

Mold after a leak

A good share of our calls come from people who found mold, not water. Mold needs moisture, so a past leak, a damp basement wall or a humid, unventilated bathroom is often behind it. Fixing the moisture comes before any cleaning.

Our mold remediation service follows ANSI/IICRC S520 practice: containing the area, removing affected materials and drying what remains. The Massachusetts DPH homeowner mold guide notes that no license is required to conduct mold remediation in the state. So ask any company how its crews work and whether they follow S520. Questions about health effects belong with your physician.

How a restoration job runs

1

Call and describe the loss

Tell the dispatcher where the water came from, what is wet and whether it is still running. Dispatch is 24/7, so the call can happen at 2 a.m.

2

Assessment and scope

The crew checks the damage with moisture meters, identifies the water category and explains the scope of work and the price before any work starts.

3

Extraction and drying

Standing water is pumped or vacuumed out, wet materials are removed where needed, and air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure. Readings are taken until the materials are dry.

4

Repair and closeout

Once everything reads dry, the crew or a repair contractor replaces drywall, flooring and finishes, and you get a record of the work done.

Insurance documentation: what to keep

Sudden and accidental water damage, like a burst pipe, is often covered by a homeowners policy, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not. Mass.gov says standard homeowners policies do not cover flood damage, though a rider for sump pump failure or water backup may give some coverage. Check your policy and ask your insurer what applies.

Whatever the cause, keep photos, video, receipts and the written scope of work. Mass.gov advises photographing damage inside and out. Do not throw away damaged items until your insurer has had a chance to see them or has told you that you can.

Marblehead conditions that shape water damage

Harbor, Neck and older houses

The town's own hazard mitigation plan says flood zones are present throughout Marblehead. The state-funded 2017-2018 climate planning report names flood-prone areas that include The Causeway to the Neck, parts of Marblehead Harbor such as Front and Gregory Streets, Little Harbor to Doliber's Cove, and Atlantic Avenue near Preston Beach. If you live in one of these areas, check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask your insurer about separate flood coverage before storm season.

That report also says The Causeway is the only road access to the Neck and was closed seven times in the year before it was written, because of storms and extreme high tides. If you live on the Neck, say so when you call, because access can be affected after a coastal storm.

Marblehead has a long record of coastal storms. The hazard plan lists a March 2018 coastal storm and a September 2023 severe weather and flooding event among its federal disaster declarations. After a nor'easter, water often arrives from outside, which means contaminated Category 3 water and a different job than a pipe break.

Old Town is largely protected by the Marblehead Historic District and has many older houses. Old framing, plaster and wood floors can hold moisture and react to heavy equipment, so tell the dispatcher the age of the house and ask the crew to explain how they will dry it. The town has a Certificate of Appropriateness process for the historic district, so ask the town before changing anything visible from the street.

One local rule matters for basements. The town says homeowners may not discharge sump pump connections into the sanitary sewer system. If you are planning a replacement pump after a flood, contact the town's Highway and Drain Department about where the discharge may go.

Sources: Town of Marblehead Hazard Mitigation Plan (Oct 2023), MA Municipal Vulnerability Preparedness Planning Grant Report 2017-2018 (mass.gov), Town of Marblehead sump pump page, Wikipedia (historic district).

Frequently Asked Questions

Is emergency water removal available at night in Marblehead?

Yes. The dispatch line is open 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays. Call, describe the water, and dispatch connects you with a licensed and insured crew. We do not promise an arrival time, but we will tell you what to do while you wait.

What should I do first when water floods my basement?

Stop the source if it is a plumbing leak, keep out of the water if it could reach outlets or the panel, and call 911 or the utility if there is an electrical or gas risk. Then call the dispatch line and take photos before moving anything.

Will you waterproof my basement?

No. Restoration is not waterproofing. We dry and repair the damage, but we do not do foundation work, sealing or exterior drainage. If water keeps coming in, you will need a waterproofing specialist in addition to restoration.

How do I know if my loss is large enough to involve my insurer?

Call your insurer early on any loss that is more than a small, quickly cleaned spill. Large losses often need an assessment by the insurer, and they will tell you what documentation they need. Keep photos, receipts and the written scope of work.

Is flood damage covered by homeowners insurance?

Usually not. Mass.gov says standard homeowners policies do not cover flood damage, and flood coverage is separate. A rider for sump pump failure or water backup may give some coverage, so check your policy.

Do you handle mold as well as water damage?

Yes. Mold remediation is our second service, after water damage. We follow S520 practice, and fixing the moisture source comes first. See our mold remediation page for the steps.

How long does drying take?

It depends on what is wet and what the material is. Dense materials like hardwood and plaster dry more slowly than carpet. Crews take moisture readings until the materials are dry, rather than working to a set number of days.
