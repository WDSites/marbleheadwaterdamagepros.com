Where Beverly's water comes from

Drinking water here comes from the Salem and Beverly Water Supply Board, which draws on the Ipswich River, Wenham Lake, Putnamville Reservoir and the Longham Reservoirs. Treatment happens at the Arlington Avenue plant in North Beverly. The board's supply is separate from your home's piping, so a problem inside your walls is yours to fix, while a main break in the street belongs to the city.

The city's Department of Public Services, 148 Park Street, runs the water division for distribution, shut-offs and low pressure. Its Highway and Sewer Division maintains catch basins, storm drains and sewer lines. If street water is rising because a drain is blocked, report it to the city, then call us for the damage inside.

The Bass River District and tidal flooding

The tidal Bass River meets the Danvers River at Salters Point, with salt marsh edging much of the river close to downtown. The state's Bass River District Resilience Plan describes a commercial and industrial district on the river's east bank, running from Elliott Street (Route 62) to Webber Avenue. It warns that sea level rise will keep worsening flood risk there.

The district includes the Cummings Center, Beverly Depot, the Margin Street pump station and a National Grid substation. Tenants and managers in those buildings should keep a plan for moving inventory and equipment up off floors, since tidal water can arrive quickly and recede slowly. Route 62 and Route 127 are listed as vulnerable roads, so access during a flood may be limited.

Low-lying streets and coastal storm history

Beverly's Municipal Vulnerability Preparedness findings name Marsh Avenue, Fosters Point, Willow Street and Quincy Park as low-lying neighborhoods. Coastal flooding was the top hazard for most workshop participants. A 2016 King Tide flooded coastal homes, and the 2017 coastal storms damaged seawalls at Lynch Park and Endicott College.

If you live in those areas, check your basement and crawl space after every high tide paired with onshore wind. Salt and brackish water corrodes furnaces and electrical panels quickly, so early emergency water removal limits what has to be replaced.

What to do before a crew reaches your Beverly address

  • Close the main shutoff and, if the water came in from outside, avoid wading through it, since floodwater and tidal water can carry contamination.
  • Do not use electrical devices in wet rooms and do not touch a panel while standing in water.
  • Move cars and stored items out of a flooding garage only if it is safe to enter.
  • Take photos of the water line on the walls before anything gets moved, then call the 24/7 line.

Older housing and what it means for repairs

The city's Housing Needs Assessment found about 41 percent of Beverly's housing predates World War II. Roughly 55 percent of homes are single-family, and about 35 percent sit in buildings of three or more units, with around 61 percent owner-occupied. Older frames, lath and plaster and original piping mean water damage cleanup often has to open walls to find where water traveled.

Beverly Farms addresses use 01915, the same as the rest of the city, so tell dispatch the village name along with the street.

Once a structure is dry, repairs begin. The Building Department at 8 Dane Street issues permits and inspects building, electrical, plumbing and gas work, so ask it before replacing framing or rewiring. Our water damage repair page explains how rebuilding follows drying.

Beverly notes from public records

Beverly's hazard mitigation plan lists four developments in the 100-year flood zone, including 46 River Street and 201 Elliott Street, among 12,572 buildings citywide. The city's Floodplain Development page says work in Special Flood Hazard Areas needs a permit and explains substantial-damage rules. Before rebuilding after a major flood, check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask the city.

In May 2006, over 12 inches of rain caused severe flooding, which later drove inland flood projects. Nearby Salem and Peabody are also on our service route if water has crossed town lines.

If water is still spreading in Beverly

Close the water off

Turn the fixture valve or the main shutoff. Find it before the water rises.

Power off if the panel is dry

Cut wet circuits only when you can reach the panel on dry footing. Otherwise call the electric utility or 911 and stay out.

Raise what matters

Shift furniture legs onto blocks, lift documents and electronics to upper shelves, and pull rugs before salt water stains set.

Avoid tidal and sewer water

Storm tide can carry sewage. Keep children and pets clear, and do not wade in it barefoot.

Take photos

Capture the waterline, the damaged contents and any visible tide marks. Time-stamped images help later with insurer questions.

Phone the 24/7 line

Call dispatch once the steps above are done. Give the street, the affected floor and whether the water is clean, brackish or sewage.

Questions From Beverly Property Owners

Do you cover every ZIP code in Beverly?

Beverly street addresses use ZIP 01915, which spans the city from the Cove and Lynch Park to Beverly Farms, Ryal Side and North Beverly. Prides Crossing mail uses a PO box ZIP with no street delivery, so use your street address and village name when you call. Beverly Farms has no separate ZIP and falls under 01915.

Is emergency water removal available in Beverly?

Yes. The dispatch line operates 24/7, including during coastal storms, and licensed and insured professionals are sent for pumping and extraction. Same-day response is offered when available. Our flood damage restoration page covers storm and tidal events.

Does a high tide that floods my basement count as flood damage for insurance?

It usually does, and standard homeowners policies typically exclude flood, so you may need separate flood coverage. Mass.gov says a rider for sump pump failure or water backup can give some coverage. Check your policy and ask your insurer.

I'm near the Bass River. Should I expect salt water to affect my equipment?

Yes. Brackish water corrodes motors, controls and panel components faster than fresh water. Keep electrical equipment above floor level when tides run high, and call before restarting anything that got wet.

What if my Beverly home is a three-decker with damage on several floors?

Tell dispatch how many units are affected. Water usually follows framing downward, so crews check each floor with moisture meters rather than guessing. Rebuilding comes after drying is confirmed.
