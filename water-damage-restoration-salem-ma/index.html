When water is spreading in Salem

Salem is a city of 44,480 people (2020 Census) that neighbors Marblehead along the Forest River, with our Essex St base in Marblehead 01945 as the dispatch point. Salem's problem is mostly water arriving from outside: tidal rivers, the harbor edge and heavy rain.

Start by working out whether the water is rising or leaking. Rising water from a river or storm drain is a flood and is contaminated. Leaking water from a pipe or water heater is usually clean at first, and that distinction drives the crew's plan.

Round-the-clock dispatch

Emergency dispatch is available 24/7 for every Salem address in 01970, storm nights included.

Water over six inches

A deep basement needs pumps before anything else. The city's own flooding page sets a six inch threshold for fire department help.

Drying starts fast

Dehumidifiers and air movers then dry framing and subfloor.

River and harbor-edge flooding

Salem's 2020 hazard mitigation plan draft lists the Canal Street neighborhood on the South River watershed, the North River and Bridge Street neighborhoods, and the Derby Wharf area as flood-prone. Collins Cove and Juniper Cove appear in the storm impact lists. If you live or rent near these tidal waters, a king tide plus rain is the combination to watch, and your storage should already be off the floor.

Downtown, Bridge Street Neck, the Point and South Salem are described as the more densely developed parts of the city. Dense blocks mean water often travels through shared walls and stairwells. Our flood damage restoration page covers that work.

The Willows and the Forest River edge

The plan draft also names Columbus Avenue at Willows Park, Daniels Street, Ocean Avenue and Willows Park, plus the Forest River at the Marblehead border. The city's flooding page notes that the Willows neighborhood was closed to non-residents during one event, so ask the dispatcher about access when you call.

The same page says an electrician and heating contractor must check utilities before they are put back into use. After a flood, do not restart the furnace or water heater on your own, and ask the contractor to confirm the work in writing.

If water is still spreading in Salem

Close the water at the main

Shut the main valve if a pipe or tank is the source. If the water is coming up from a drain or the ground, closing the main will not stop it, so move to the next steps.

Leave a wet panel alone

If the basement panel is wet or you would have to wade to reach it, do not touch it. Call 911 or the utility and let them cut service.

Get valuables above the line

Carry papers, laptops and photo boxes to a higher floor. In a multi-unit building, ask the upstairs neighbor to check their ceilings too.

Stay out of dirty water

River and storm water in a basement can be contaminated. Keep children and pets upstairs and wear boots if you must cross it.

Record the damage

Photograph the water level against a wall, the source and each ruined item.

Call before the water rises further

Call from a dry room and say whether the water is clean, how deep it is and whether the basement has an outside door.

Basement pump-outs and when to call whom

According to the city's flooding page, the Salem Fire Department pumps out basements holding more than six inches of water when it is safe to do so. That is an emergency stopgap, not a restoration. Once the water is out, wet drywall, insulation and flooring still have to be removed and dried, which is where emergency water removal and drying crews come in.

The 2020 plan draft also notes that sewer pump stations can flood. If sewage comes up through a floor drain after a storm, treat it as a sanitation event: keep out of the area and call us promptly.

Older housing and a rental city

The city's plan draft reports 19,399 housing units (ACS 2017) with 56.2% built before 1940 and 50.9% renter-occupied. The Census (2020 to 2024) counts 49.7% of Salem households as owners. Around Salem Common, the city's oldest park dating to 1803 and part of a National Register historic district, the older houses were built long before modern drainage, so ask for moisture readings in walls.

Tenants should report a leak to the landlord in writing. Also see water damage cleanup for what happens after the water is out. Check your policy for sudden and accidental water damage and for flood.

Water supply, sewers and permits

Salem's drinking water comes from the Ipswich River and three reservoirs (Wenham Lake, Putnamville and Longham), treated at a plant in Beverly run by the Salem/Beverly Water Supply Board. Wastewater goes to the South Essex Sewerage District plant at 50 Fort Avenue, and Marblehead is a co-permittee of that same plant.

Building, plumbing and gas permits go through Salem's Inspectional Services. Ask them before rebuilding walls.

The plan draft counts 17 repetitive-loss structures in Salem with 46 losses and $702,208.16 in claims paid. For a property that has flooded before, keep dated photos and invoices ready for the next claim.

Salem notes and nearby coverage

We also serve Swampscott and Lynn. Mold can follow a wet basement, and mold remediation is its own scope with its own standard, ANSI/IICRC S520.

Questions From Salem Property Owners

Do you cover every ZIP code in Salem?

Salem's service ZIP is 01970, and we cover it in full. That runs from Salem Common through Bridge Street, Canal Street and the Willows to the Forest River at the Marblehead border.

Is emergency water removal available in Salem?

Yes, at any hour. The dispatch line stays open and a crew can go out the same day when one is available.

Will the Salem fire department pump my basement?

The city's flooding page says the department pumps basements with more than six inches of water when it is safe. It does not dry or restore the space, so you still need a restoration crew afterward.

Is Canal Street really that flood-prone?

The 2020 hazard mitigation plan draft lists the Canal Street neighborhood on the South River watershed as flood-prone. If you are there, ask your insurer about flood coverage separately, since standard homeowners policies generally exclude it.

Which Salem office handles repair permits?

Salem's Inspectional Services handles building, plumbing and gas permits. Ask them whether your repair needs one, especially for structural or mechanical work.
