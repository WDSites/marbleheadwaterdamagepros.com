When water is spreading in Peabody

Peabody is a city of about 54,000 people in Essex County, and Marblehead Water Damage Pros serves it from a base in Marblehead on the North Shore. The dispatch line answers around the clock, including nights, weekends and holidays. Water that sits in framing and insulation keeps spreading, so the first call matters more than the second.

Most calls fit one of three patterns below.

Emergency dispatch, day or night

The line is staffed 24/7. A 3 a.m. supply-line failure gets the same attention as a Tuesday afternoon call.

Brook and street water

Flooding near Proctor, Goldthwaite and Strongwater Brooks can enter low basements through floor drains and foundation cracks.

Pipes and appliances

Water heaters, washing machine hoses and frozen supply lines cause most indoor floods in older Peabody houses.

Three March storms, brook overflow and spring melt

The state's 2017-2018 Municipal Vulnerability Preparedness report for Peabody records that three winter storms in March 2018 caused severe flooding from rainfall and storm surge. When storms stack up, the ground is already saturated when the next one lands. If your basement took water that month, expect hidden damp in sill plates and the bottoms of finished walls.

Proctor Brook, Goldthwaite Brook and Strongwater Brook merge into the North River and then flow on to Salem. The report blames undersized, old or clogged drainage and heavy paved cover. Homes near these channels should check basements after any hard rain or fast snowmelt.

Frozen ground sheds melt toward foundations, and a sump pump idle all winter may fail on its first big run. Test yours in March with a bucket of water, and call if the float sticks.

Peabody Square and the low downtown

The state's Peabody Community Snapshot lists the North River Canal corridor in Peabody Square as a flood project area, with a resilient wall and bank stabilization meant to add flood storage. The MVP report likewise lists a low-lying town square among its flooding sites. Ground-floor tenants and landlords downtown should plan for water entering from the street side.

Street floodwater counts as contaminated, which changes how flood damage restoration is carried out. Photograph the waterline, lift stock and files, and call early.

Older houses, basements and aging pipes

The city's Housing Production Plan counts 22,530 housing units, and 4,920 of them (21.8 percent) were built in 1939 or earlier. About 55 percent are single-family homes and roughly 42 percent sit in buildings of two or more units. Older plumbing and foundations let small leaks hide for months.

In two-family buildings, water from an upstairs unit travels down into the apartments below. Tell the dispatcher how many floors are affected so the right water mitigation crew is sent.

Peabody was the Leather City, and tanneries once lined Ottoman Street. Buildings that date from that era may have deep, uneven basements, where moisture meters help find water a quick look would miss.

Who handles what in the city

The Department of Public Services oversees roadways, drainage, water supply and wastewater collection, so call it about street flooding, a sewer smell outside or a water main break. The Building Department at City Hall, 24 Lowell Street, handles inspections and permits, and permit applications go through its online system. Ask it before rebuilding walls or electrical work after a major loss.

Peabody Municipal Light Plant is the electric utility. If water has reached outlets or the panel and you cannot tell whether it is safe, call the light plant or 911 before you touch anything. Our line is for the water, not the wiring.

Peabody notes from public records

Peabody's drinking water comes mainly from the Walsh and Winona Water Treatment Plants, supplemented by MWRA water in high-demand months, per the MWRA community letter. Peabody borders Salem and is a few miles from Marblehead. The adjacent town of Danvers uses ZIP 01923, which is not part of this page. Beverly, another North Shore city we serve, has its own service page.

If water is still spreading in Peabody

Stop the source

Close the fixture valve or the main shutoff, usually where the line enters near the water meter.

Cut power only if safe

If the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water, switch off the wet circuits. If not, stay out and call the utility or 911.

Lift what you can

Move rugs, boxes and electronics onto counters or upper floors. Tenants should check the unit below as well.

Keep people and pets clear

Brook or sewer water carries contamination. Keep children and animals out of it, and wear boots if you must cross.

Photograph everything

Shoot wide and close images of the waterline, damaged items and the source before anything moves. Your insurer may ask for them.

Call the 24/7 line

Once the immediate steps are done, call dispatch. Say which floor is wet and whether the water came from inside or outside.

Questions From Peabody Property Owners

Do you cover every ZIP code in Peabody?

Peabody's street addresses use ZIP 01960, which covers the whole city from the downtown near Peabody Square to the Northshore Mall area on Andover Street. ZIP 01961 is a PO box ZIP and has no street delivery, so it is not a service address. If your mailing address says West Peabody or South Peabody, it is still 01960.

Is emergency water removal available in Peabody?

Yes. The dispatch line is open 24/7, and we send licensed and insured professionals for standing water, burst pipes and storm intrusion. Same-day response is offered when available. See our emergency water removal page for what happens on arrival.

My Peabody basement floods during heavy rain. Is that a restoration job or a drainage job?

Both can be true. We remove the water and dry the structure, but the cause may be a failed sump pump, a clogged floor drain or street runoff. For street drainage, the Department of Public Services is the city contact. Keep photos of each flood so patterns are clear.

Will insurance cover brook or storm flooding in Peabody?

Check your policy. Sudden, accidental water damage is often covered, while flood damage is usually excluded and needs separate flood coverage. Mass.gov notes a rider for sump pump failure or water backup may give some coverage. Ask your insurer before assuming either way.

Can you work on a two-family or triple-decker where water came from upstairs?

Yes. Water from one unit often reaches ceilings and walls below. Tell the dispatcher how many units are wet, then see our water damage cleanup page for how affected floors are handled one at a time.
